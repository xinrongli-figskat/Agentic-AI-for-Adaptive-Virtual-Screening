# Measurement effort in the TNAP source deposition

In this project, measurement effort means the number of independent concentration–response determinations recorded for each tested compound in the source deposition. It does not mean the number of compounds in a congeneric series. The observed per-compound count had a median of 1 and a maximum of 21.

Measurement effort predicted the final measured active/inactive class with ROC-AUC 0.879. The strongest individual structural descriptor reached ROC-AUC 0.598. The source records are consistent with selective follow-up: compounds that appeared promising received more concentration–response characterization. Thus, measurement effort records how much follow-up work a compound received and is strongly associated with the result. It is a selection and measurement-process bias, not a chemical property.

This association may coincide with chemical-series membership because promising members of a series can receive additional follow-up. The available analysis does not show that series membership is the main cause: structural descriptors alone were substantially less predictive than measurement effort. The result should therefore be described as a strong association with selective follow-up, while the causal contribution of series membership remains unresolved.

Any candidate built from a screening-database deposition should be checked for this association. Measurement effort must not be supplied as a molecular feature. The observed association is an interpretation limitation even when the selection system hides this field.
